# Fictional walkthrough: evidence before preparation

**Everything in this walkthrough is fictional.** The opportunity, organization, candidate, evidence, and outcome are invented examples. They are not a candidate record, application, employer, résumé, or private system extract.

## Scenario

Fictional organization **Northline Community Services** posts an opportunity for an **AI Operations Program Lead**. The role calls for governed workflow design, cross-functional delivery with technical teams, practical evaluation methods, and a preference for direct production-software ownership.

The fictional candidate is **Jordan Vale**. Jordan’s approved evidence supports leading operational-change programs, defining AI workflow governance, and coordinating with engineering. It does not support claiming hands-on production-software ownership.

## 1. Normalize and verify

The opportunity is converted into a consistent record with its source, title, organization, and requirements kept distinct. A duplicate with the same identifying facts would be treated as the same opportunity rather than as a second independent lead.

This matters because a later evaluation should trace to one verified opportunity, not to a copied description with uncertain provenance.

## 2. Extract requirements and compare governed evidence

| Requirement | Public-safe evaluation | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Design governed AI-enabled workflows | **DIRECT** — approved evidence supports workflow governance and human-review design. | Eligible as a supported preparation claim. |
| Partner with engineering on implementation direction | **TRANSFERABLE** — evidence supports technical-program coordination, not authorship of production software. | May be described precisely as coordination and direction. |
| Learn the organization’s evaluation tooling | **LEARNABLE** — the tool is new, but the underlying evaluation discipline is adjacent. | Can be framed as a learning plan, not an existing credential. |
| Own production distributed-service implementation | **MATERIAL** — current evidence does not establish this hands-on depth. | Lowers priority and prevents an unsupported claim. |

No rule turns an appealing phrase into evidence. The comparison uses only approved evidence and capability interpretation; unsupported material stays unsupported.

## 3. Deterministic evaluation and prioritization

The direct and transferable evidence makes the opportunity potentially relevant. The material implementation gap means it does not receive the same priority as a role centered on governance and program direction.

The useful behavior is not a hidden score. It is an inspectable consequence: keep the opportunity available for human review, record the limitation, and do not let general technical fluency masquerade as production-engineering evidence.

## 4. Preparation without invention

Preparation may assemble a review-oriented brief that:

- highlights governed workflow and evaluation experience;
- states technical-program collaboration accurately;
- identifies the production-implementation requirement as a material gap; and
- asks the reviewer whether the role’s depth requirement is essential.

It may not claim that Jordan built distributed services, held a credential not in evidence, or met a requirement simply because the language sounds adjacent.

## 5. Human review remains the boundary

A human decides whether to pursue the opportunity, revise the preparation, or stop. Final external submission is not automated at this public checkpoint.

## What this demonstrates

The scenario shows reusable system patterns: verified identity, evidence provenance, explicit gap classification, deterministic decision consequences, and human authority over consequential action. It does not demonstrate broad autonomous search or automated submission.
